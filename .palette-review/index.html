Palette exploration: what happens if the site paints less teal

Eight variants of the same homepage strip. Same content, same layout, same four surface steps, same token architecture. Only the palette values change. The logo gradient is untouched in every one.

What the built site actually paints today, measured

  • 21.5 million sampled pixels across six built routes at 1440px. 69.2% is neutral ground, 29.3% is tinted (hairlines, muted type, washes), and only 1.51% is a hue a reader could name.
  • Of that painted 1.51%: violet 59%, teal 32%, cyan 5%, blue 3%, orchid 1%. Green is 0.000%. The owner's word "green" does not describe anything on the site; every green-reading pixel is teal at 172°.
  • But the instinct is right, and the reason is monopoly rather than area. Violet's 59% is almost entirely two places (the homepage hero field and the blog duotone photographs). Strip those and teal is 67–88% of all painted colour on every other route — 67% on /pricing, 84% on /crowmark, 88% on /compare, 69% on /sources. Teal is not excessive by area. It is excessive by being the only accent.
  • And it is not carrying its meaning. 379 teal token references in astro/src. At most 15% are a verified marker. 28% paint interactive states — focus rings, hovers, links, buttons — which the charter assigns to cyan. 19% are atmosphere and decoration. The remaining 39% are bare color: var(--c-teal) on /faq, /roadmap, /sectors, /glossary, /partners, /cookie-preferences and the legal layout, where nothing is being verified at all.
  • So the collision with "teal = verified" is not real. Teal is the site's default accent, not its marker. Reducing teal does not cost meaning; it is the only way to restore it.

V2 — Neutral-dominant, teal + violet

Owner's own brief. White, black, teal, violet. Nothing else is painted anywhere.

What it does to the semantic system

Two hues carry the two semantic axes: teal = verified, violet = refused. Cyan, pink, blue and green leave the system entirely. Interactive moves to violet so teal appears ONLY on a verified mark.

Collision, stated rather than hidden: interactive and refused are the SAME violet. A hover and a refusal would read identically. Fixable by moving refused to orchid #E4A3FF, but it is shown as built so the flaw is visible.

V3 — Violet-led, teal markers only

Violet becomes the brand and the interactive colour. Teal survives as a marker and nowhere else.

V4 — Teal out of the semantic system

The hard test. Verified is carried by WHITE; teal appears only in the logo.

What it does to the semantic system

Verified = white, which is the strongest thing on a near-black page and needs no hue at all. Refused = orchid. Risk = amber. THE COST IS REAL: white already carries body copy and headings, so a white tick has to earn its meaning from FORM alone. Included so the owner can see the cost rather than be told about it.
