UK public procurement

The engine that refuses to make things up.

The hardest market to bluff in is the one with a published rulebook, so it is the one the engine is proved against. CrowAgent reads both UK registers, drafts answers cited to the published requirement, to your own past bids and to commitments you have confirmed, and evidences delivery after award.

The same engine runs a private-sector bid. What changes is the rulebook it cites, not the standard of proof.

Refusal gate

A figure with nothing behind it does not survive the draft.

Every pound and every percentage in a CrowAgent answer is computed from a commitment you have confirmed. If the commitment is not there, the claim is taken out before a named human reviews the answer.

How it runs

Five stages, and one of them is a refusal.

The gate is not a filter bolted on at the end. It sits between the draft and the human who signs it, and it is the only stage whose job is to remove work rather than produce it.

  1. Stage 01

    Find

    Find a Tender and Contracts Finder, read continuously and scored against the evidence you hold.

  2. Stage 02

    Qualify

    Fit, component by component, with the reason for each. Stated as context, never as a probability of award.

  3. Stage 03

    Draft

    Answers grounded in your answer library, the published requirement and your confirmed commitments.

  4. Stage 04

    Refuse

    Any figure without a recorded commitment behind it is taken out of the draft before review.

  5. Stage 05

    Evidence

    After award, each commitment is tracked to the dated evidence that proves it, month by month.

Notices from Find a Tender and Contracts Finder, scored against what you can evidence today

01 · Supplier · Discover

Notices you could actually win the argument on.

Both UK registers are read: Find a Tender and Contracts Finder. Relevance is scored against the evidence you already hold rather than against the words in the notice, and the register a notice came from is named on the notice, so the trail out to the source is one click and not a search.

Relevance is a sorting aid. It is not a forecast of anything.

app.crowagent.ai/mark/qualify

Fit broken down component by component, stated as context and never as a probability of award

02 · Supplier · Bid or no bid

Fit, broken into its parts, with the reason attached.

The score is decomposed component by component, each with a band, a points total and a one-line reason you can argue with. What it is not is stated on the screen itself, in the product, where a reader cannot miss it.

“This is FIT context. It is not a probability of award, and CrowMark does not produce one.”

app.crowagent.ai/mark/draft

Award questions with their weightings, above a drafter told where every figure has to come from

03 · Supplier · Draft

The drafter is told where a figure may come from.

The tender is split into its scored questions, each with its category, word limit and weighting. The boundary the drafter works inside runs across the top of the screen and is not a setting anyone can turn off.

“Answers are AI-assisted and grounded only in your own answer library, the published requirement and your confirmed commitments. Every £ and % comes from your engine-computed commitments and is never invented. A named human reviews, edits and approves every answer before you submit it.”

app.crowagent.ai/mark/evaluate

The passage that answers the criterion, located and cited, with the band left to a named evaluator

04 · Buyer · Evaluate

It finds the passage. It does not set the band.

One criterion against one response. The supplier is quoted verbatim and the quote is cited to a section and a page of the response document, so an evaluator checks a reference rather than trusting a summary. The band, and the audit line under it, belong to a named person.

“CrowMark locates the passage that answers this criterion and shows you where it came from. It does not score, rank or recommend. The band is yours to set.”

The evaluation banner, on the screen above

app.crowagent.ai/mark/delivery

What each supplier committed at award, against what they have since evidenced

05 · Buyer · After award

A commitment is only worth the evidence filed against it.

Promises made at award are carried forward as measures, and each measure collects dated evidence against it month by month. The same view carries the next assessment date for each contract, which is the part of the duty that is easiest to lose track of and the hardest to explain later.

Each contract reads on track, at risk or behind against the evidence received, not against a projection. Suppliers see the other half of this: the same commitments, with the upload that proves each one.

Both sides of the table

Read from either end.

The supplier writing the answer and the authority evaluating it are working on the same passage. It should be the same passage, cited the same way, on both screens.
