Palette exploration: what happens if the site paints less teal

Eight variants of the same homepage strip. Same content, same layout, same four surface steps, same token architecture. Only the palette values change. The logo gradient is untouched in every one.

Added 4 August: eleven institutional & AI-recommended variants, E1–E11, which leave teal behind entirely or ration it strictly, arguing instead for institutional seriousness and assurance clarity. Those eleven are rendered live in the browser rather than as images, so what you are looking at is the colour itself.

What the built site actually paints today, measured

  • 21.5 million sampled pixels across six built routes at 1440px. 69.2% is neutral ground, 29.3% is tinted (hairlines, muted type, washes), and only 1.51% is a hue a reader could name.
  • Of that painted 1.51%: violet 59%, teal 32%, cyan 5%, blue 3%, orchid 1%. Green is 0.000%. The owner's word "green" does not describe anything on the site; every green-reading pixel is teal at 172°.
  • But the instinct is right, and the reason is monopoly rather than area. Violet's 59% is almost entirely two places (the homepage hero field and the blog duotone photographs). Strip those and teal is 67–88% of all painted colour on every other route — 67% on /pricing, 84% on /crowmark, 88% on /compare, 69% on /sources. Teal is not excessive by area. It is excessive by being the only accent.
  • And it is not carrying its meaning. 379 teal token references in astro/src. At most 15% are a verified marker. 28% paint interactive states — focus rings, hovers, links, buttons — which the charter assigns to cyan. 19% are atmosphere and decoration. The remaining 39% are bare color: var(--c-teal) on /faq, /roadmap, /sectors, /glossary, /partners, /cookie-preferences and the legal layout, where nothing is being verified at all.
  • So the collision with "teal = verified" is not real. Teal is the site's default accent, not its marker. Reducing teal does not cost meaning; it is the only way to restore it.

Six more, asked for on 4 August: colour that argues for seriousness rather than about how much teal

V0–V7 above all kept teal somewhere and only argued about how much of it there should be. These six leave it. Each is a complete system rather than a swapped accent: a ground and three surface steps, a decorative hairline and a separate control border, three text weights, an accent with its own hover/active and focus values, a link colour, a filled control with its label, and a semantic set that belongs to the rest of it.

Every pair is computed, not eyeballed. 44 pairs per variant — every text role against all four surfaces, both button states, and every control boundary and focus ring against every surface it can land on. 264 pairs in total; all 264 pass WCAG 2.2 AA (4.5:1 text, 3:1 non-text). Four candidates failed on the first pass and their values were moved until they passed; the source that recomputes all of this on every run is .palette-review/institutional-variants.mjs.

Each accent and semantic set is then run through a Viénot/Brettel dichromat simulation for deuteranopia and protanopia, and every pair a reader must tell apart is scored by CIE ΔE on the simulated colours. Nothing in the semantic set carries meaning by colour alone: confirmed, at risk and refused each have a glyph and a word, which is what the specimen shows.

These are live specimens, not renders — a real heading, real body copy, a real link, a primary and a secondary button, a permanently-shown focus ring, a card on a raised surface with a real control border, a hairline rule, an eyebrow chip and the three semantic states. Nothing in astro/ or on :8095 has been touched.

What it signals

Navy is the colour of the official record in this country — Companies House, the gilt-edged market, the civil service estate, the cover of anything that has been filed rather than launched. Brass is the seal on top of it. It suits a procurement audience because it is the palette an evaluator already associates with an authority that keeps records, not a vendor that ships features; and gold rations itself, because gold at any density stops reading as a seal and starts reading as a casino. The heading, body and card furniture are carried entirely by the navy ladder, so the brass appears perhaps six times on a page and is noticed every time.

Weakness. It is the most heavily worn serious palette there is. Navy and gold is the default of every private bank, every magic-circle firm and a great many things pretending to be both, so it buys credibility at a real cost in distinctiveness — nobody will remember it. And the brass sits about fifteen degrees from the amber that carries "at risk", which means the warning state cannot rely on its hue and must always be a glyph plus a word.

What it signals

Graphite with no blue in it at all is the single hardest ground to make look cheap, because there is no hue to get wrong — it reads as printed matter, bound reports, a set of chambers. Oxblood is unusual in software and instantly legible as legal and serious, and crucially it works here as a material rather than a tint: it fills the primary button, rules the section divides and stamps the seal, with white doing the interactive work on top of it. For a buyer reading a compliance claim, a palette that looks like a legal opinion is doing useful work before a word is read.

Weakness. A brand built on dark red cannot then use red to mean danger. Danger has to shift to a lighter vermilion and carry a glyph in every instance, and even so those two reds are the closest pair anywhere in this exploration. There is a second, quieter cost: oxblood at its proper depth is too dark to be text on a dark ground at 4.5:1, so the brand colour appears less often than a brand colour normally does — its text form is a muted claret that is noticeably pinker than the fill, and the two have to be understood as one colour by an audience that has no reason to.

What it signals

Bottle green with bone and gilt is about as British-establishment as a palette gets — the racing car, the leather of the reading room, the spine of a bound volume. Against a market where every bid tool is either blue or teal on grey, it is genuinely distinctive, and it carries age, which is exactly the thing a two-year-old company selling assurance would like to borrow. The bone-white type instead of pure white is the detail that does most of the work: it removes the screen-glare quality of #FFF and makes the whole page read as printed.

Weakness. It does not solve the owner's complaint, it renames it. The site reads greenish today because teal is everywhere; a bottle-green ground reads green on purpose, and an audience that thought "sustainability" will go on thinking it — arguably more so, since green is now the ground rather than an accent. It is also the weakest variant for colour vision: a green ground under a green-blind eye flattens toward the same grey as the success state, so success must never be green here without a tick beside it.
