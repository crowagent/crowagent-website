The product

One tender, from notice to evidence.

Five screens along the same contract: finding the notice, deciding whether to bid, drafting the answers, evaluating the responses, and proving delivery afterwards. The wording each screen carries is quoted beside it, because the wording is the argument.

Notices you could actually win the argument on.

05 · Buyer · After award

A commitment is only worth the evidence filed against it.

Promises made at award are carried forward as measures, and each measure collects dated evidence against it month by month. The same view carries the next assessment date for each contract, which is the part of the duty that is easiest to lose track of and the hardest to explain later.

Each contract reads on track, at risk or behind against the evidence received, not against a projection.

Suppliers see the other half of this: the same commitments, with the upload that proves each one.

How it runs

Five stages, and one of them is a refusal.

The gate is not a filter bolted on at the end. It sits between the draft and the human who signs it, and it is the only stage whose job is to remove work rather than produce it.

  1. Stage 01
    Find

    Find a Tender and Contracts Finder, read continuously and scored against the evidence you hold.

  2. Stage 02
    Qualify

    Fit, component by component, with the reason for each. Stated as context, never as a probability of award.

  3. Stage 03
    Draft

    Answers grounded in your answer library, the published requirement and your confirmed commitments.

  4. Stage 04
    Refuse

    Any figure without a recorded commitment behind it is taken out of the draft before review.

  5. Stage 05
    Evidence

    After award, each commitment is tracked to the dated evidence that proves it, month by month.

Both sides

Built for both ends of the table.

The supplier writing the answer and the authority evaluating it are working on the same passage. It should be the same passage, cited the same way, on both screens.

Suppliers answering

Answers with the source attached.

Qualify a notice against what you can evidence today, draft inside a boundary that names your own answer library and your confirmed commitments as the only sources, and carry the commitments you made into delivery rather than rediscovering them a year later.

  • Fit given component by component, with a reason for each
  • Your own submitted answers held as the sources a new draft is grounded in
  • Every duty with a date on it in one queue, on the device it will be remembered on
Buyers evaluating

Bands set by people, passages found by software.

Publish criteria with the evidence each one demands, have the answering passage located and cited to a section and page, then band it yourself. Four bands, no half marks and no rounding, and every disagreement of more than one band goes to moderation.

  • Criteria and weightings set against the PPN 002 minimum
  • Committed measures quoted from each response, neither normalised nor ranked
  • Every band against the person who set it and the moment they set it
The core principle

If a figure cannot be traced, the engine refuses it.

A generative tool asked for a number it does not have will produce a plausible one. CrowAgent does the opposite, and it does it visibly: the claim comes out of the draft, and the reason it came out is recorded next to where it used to be.

Grounding

It reads evidence where the evidence already is.

Nothing here is a new place to file things. The sources are the ones your organisation already keeps, plus the two public registers.

Your own past bids

Answers you have already submitted, held as the sources a new draft is grounded in. Answers marked as coming from a won bid rank higher as sources.

The published requirement

The tender itself, and the two UK registers it was published on: Find a Tender and Contracts Finder. Each notice keeps the link back to the register it came from.

Your confirmed commitments

The measures you committed to at award, and the dated evidence filed against them afterwards. What you proved last year is what you may cite this year.
